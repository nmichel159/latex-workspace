## What the paper does

- Defines a hyper-heuristic as search over a heuristic space H for h* minimizing a meta-objective F(h), and a Language Hyper-Heuristic (LHH) as one whose heuristics are generated by LLMs (Definitions 3.1-3.2). F = mean performance on a set of instances.
- ReEvo (section 4): individuals are code snippets with a fixed function signature; two LLM roles, generator and reflector. One iteration: random parent pairs (distinct F), short-term reflection on why one parent is better, crossover using that reflection, long-term reflection that accumulates hints, elitist mutation of the current best.
- Applications (section 5): penalty heuristics for guided local search on TSP; heuristic measures for ant colony optimization on TSP, CVRP, OP, MKP, BPP; genetic operators for an electronic-design problem (decap placement); constructive TSP heuristics on TSPLIB; attention reshaping for neural solvers (POMO, LEHD).
- Analysis (section 6): defines the neighborhood of a heuristic as the outputs an LLM produces with non-negligible probability for a given prompt (Definition 6.1), and measures landscape ruggedness by autocorrelation and correlation length (Definitions 6.2-6.3). Reflection gives a longer correlation length, i.e. a smoother landscape (Table 4). Ablations: every component helps on ACO for TSP100 (Table 5).
- Comparison with EoH on five problems with three LLMs, black-box prompts, three runs: ReEvo is more sample-efficient (Fig. 4).

## Difference from our work

ReEvo tunes components of existing metaheuristics on instance distributions; it does not characterize which instance classes
make a component good, which is the kind of statement a graph-theoretic analysis provides.

## Experiments

- Settings (Appendix C, Table 8): gpt-3.5-turbo as generator and reflector, temperature 1 (+0.3 at initialization), population 10, 30 initial individuals, at most 100 heuristic evaluations, crossover rate 1, mutation rate 0.5.
- Runs: 3 per setting; best-on-validation heuristic tested on 64 held-out instances (Appendix C).
- Cost: about USD 0.06 per run with GPT-3.5 Turbo; about USD 0.0003 per call (section 7, Appendix C). Hardware for timing: one core of AMD EPYC 7742, one RTX 3090.
- Dispersion: mean +- standard deviation in Tables 4-5; three runs in Fig. 2 and Fig. 4. No hypothesis tests in the parts read.
- Limitation stated by the authors: budget of 100 evaluations; results "do not necessarily scale up", and with many evaluations the effect of reflection may vanish, citing `Zhang2024EvolutionarySearch` (section 7).

## Doubts and open questions

- Several baseline numbers are taken from other papers (marked in Tables 1-2), not re-run under the same budget.
- Model identifiers are given as family names (gpt-3.5-turbo, GPT-4 Turbo, Llama 3 70B) without snapshot dates in the parts read.
