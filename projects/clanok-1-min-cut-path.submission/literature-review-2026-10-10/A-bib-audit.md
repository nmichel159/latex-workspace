## 4. Task 3 - textbook source

**Book record (Crossref 10.1007/978-3-319-21275-3).** Authors in order: Marek Cygan, Fedor V. Fomin, Łukasz Kowalik, Daniel Lokshtanov, Dániel Marx, Marcin Pilipczuk, Michał Pilipczuk, Saket Saurabh. Title "Parameterized Algorithms". Publisher Springer International Publishing, Cham. Issued 2015. ISBNs 9783319212746 (print) and 9783319212753 (electronic; this is the ISBN-13 978-3-319-21275-3 of the DOI; check digit valid). The Springer page itself was not opened (redirect to a cookie-authorization endpoint, not followed). The authors' companion page (Wayback snapshot 2025-08-15 of www.mimuw.edu.pl/~malcin/book/) says a free PDF copy may be downloaded for personal use; the host answers 403 to my client, so I read the Wayback snapshot of that PDF (2025-07-01), 629 PDF pages, streamed into memory, not saved. This free PDF may differ in pagination from the printed Springer edition; I compared nothing against print.

**a) Definition of FPT with computable f.** Definition 1.2, printed p. 13 (Section 1.1 "Formal definitions"): a parameterized problem L is fixed-parameter tractable if some algorithm decides (x,k) in time f(k)·|(x,k)|^c for a computable function f and a constant c. The text right after it explains that f is assumed computable to avoid trouble in the complexity theory, and that f may be assumed nondecreasing. Definition 1.3 (XP) uses two computable functions.

**b) Courcelle's theorem.** Theorem 7.11 (Courcelle's theorem, attributed to Courcelle 1990), printed p. 183, Section 7.4.2. Content: for an MSO2 formula phi and an n-vertex graph G equipped with the evaluation of all free variables of phi, with a tree decomposition of G of width t provided, an algorithm decides whether phi holds in G within the time bound "in time f(||ϕ||,t)·n, for some computable function f"; ||phi|| is defined just above it as the length of the encoding of phi as a string. The free-variables feature is stated again on p. 184: both Theorem 7.11 and the optimization version Theorem 7.12 allow extra free variables whose evaluation is given with the graph, useful when the graph comes with predefined objects such as terminals (this covers vertex labels as free set variables; the book does not use the word "label" for it). MSO2 (quantification over vertices and edges, and sets of both) is stronger than the vertex-only MSO of Marx et al.'s Theorem 2.2. Remark on p. 183 after the theorem: the requirement that a tree decomposition be given is not necessary, since an optimal decomposition can be computed within the same complexity bounds (details in the next section).

**c) Tree decompositions in FPT time.** Theorem 7.17 (Bodlaender, p. 191): an algorithm that, for an n-vertex graph G and integer k, runs in time k^{O(k^3)}·n and either builds a tree decomposition of width at most k or concludes tw(G) > k. Theorem 7.18 (p. 191): an algorithm with time O(8^k k^2 n^2) that either builds a tree decomposition of width at most 4k+4 or concludes tw(G) > k. The book also remarks on p. 191 that a 5-approximation in time 2^{O(k)} n exists but is beyond its scope. So a decomposition of width O(t) is computed in FPT time by Theorem 7.18 (width at most 4k+4, time quadratic in n), and an exact one, linear in n, by Theorem 7.17. Combined with Theorem 7.11 this removes the assumption of a given decomposition; the book says so in words (remark on p. 183) and does not state the combined bound as a numbered theorem. The combined bound is therefore my inference from Theorems 7.11 and 7.17, not a quoted statement.

**d) Marx et al. text.** Both arXiv v1 (p. 1) and the author's manuscript (p. 0:1) define FPT in the introduction as solvability in time f(k)·n^{O(1)} where f is a computable function depending only on the parameter, citing Downey-Fellows, Flum-Grohe, Niedermeier. Their Theorem 2.2 states the time as f_phi(tw(G))·(|E(G)|+|V(G)|) without saying that f_phi is computable and without stating that a tree decomposition is computed (it does not say how the width bound is used algorithmically); the labeled-graph remark has the same form. So for the manuscript sentence in `main.tex` (line 1323, FPT with computable f, cited to Marx et al.) the definition is on p. 1 of the arXiv text; for the "computable" in the Courcelle bound (line 1456) the Marx text is silent and Cygan et al., Theorem 7.11, supplies it.
