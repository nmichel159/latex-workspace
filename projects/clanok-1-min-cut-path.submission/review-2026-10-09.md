## 0. Verdict in ten lines

1. The results are correct as far as I could check: I followed the reduction, both structural theorems and the
   random-graph section step by step and found no false statement.
2. The weakest part is the write-up of Section 3.1: the construction is defined in five places and the proof of
   Theorem 3.5 mixes pseudocode, definitions and argument.
3. Two lemmas of Section 4 have no proof, and the count in Theorem 4.5 carries a term that is always zero.
4. Section 4 silently needs simple graphs; the article never says "simple".
5. "Cut" is used with two meanings and "cut-value" is never defined.
6. Section 6 proves an upper bound that is never used and states its main theorem through a definition that does
   not fit random graphs.
7. The quoted random-graph theorems cite whole books; one of them (Theorem 6.2) needs its exact form checked.
8. Related work is four citations long and does not back "has not been studied before".
9. Two cheap results are missing: the 2-approximation that follows from Lemma 2.4 and the reading of
   Separating Shortest Path as "cp = d".
10. Front and back matter still lack funding, the AI declaration and highlights.

### 4.3 The proof of Theorem 3.5

- **[M] "Shortest paths" (l.589-593).** Correct. It uses that a connecting path has at least `Lambda` edges and
  that `Q` needs one more edge; the reason given ("no connecting path joins u and v directly") holds because
  every thread has at least two crossing edges. Say that.
- **[M] "Synchronization" (l.595-605).** Correct; I checked both pairs of threads against the four sign
  combinations.
- **[M] "Clause threads" (l.607-610).** Correct.
- **[M] Direction (b), "P is separating" (l.627-645).** Correct, but written as a walk: "all ways in which a
  path starting at u could progress". Completeness of the cases rests on the reader. Item 2 ends with "leads
  back to u or to a dead end"; item 3 handles one hop from a clause thread and leaves the repetition along the
  clause thread implicit.
  Proposal (Lemma D): define the set `X` of vertices consisting of `u`, the first connecting path of every
  thread, the unused paths of all `I_i`, for every clause the unused paths of its literal links that precede
  its first satisfied literal together with the clause-thread segments between them, and every connecting path
  that ends at a crossing edge on `P` and starts in `X`. Show that every edge of `G \setminus P` with one end in

  A remark that helps the reader: hitting every thread is necessary but not sufficient in general; a path may
  change threads on an unused path. The 3-synchronization threads are placed so that every such change ends at
  a crossing edge on `P`.
- **[M] Running time (l.648-654).** Correct: `2n + 7m` threads, `Lambda = O(n + m)`, `O((n + m)^2)` edges.

- **[M] Possible strengthening.** In the constructed graph every vertex other than `u` and `v` has degree at
